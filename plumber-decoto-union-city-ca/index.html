The area

Decoto before and after 1959

BART's station page says Union City combined the communities of Alvarado, Decoto and New Haven when it incorporated in 1959. The Washington Township Museum of Local History traces the name of Decoto to a pioneer family of the same name. Wikipedia adds that a settlement grew around a railroad stop on land the family sold in 1867.

The Alvarado and Decoto school districts combined in 1965 to form the New Haven Unified School District, according to the museum. Decoto Road is the main street that carries the name today. The city's older Decoto Industrial Park Study Area plan, adopted in 1994, was replaced by the Station District Specific Plan, so the name still turns up in city planning documents.

We do not claim an exact boundary for Decoto, because the city does not publish one. If your street is near Decoto Road, the water and sewer providers are the same as everywhere in Union City. For the area around BART, see our page on the Station District.

ACWD guidance

The water service line, explained

The Alameda County Water District owns the pipe on the street side of the meter. The owner owns everything from the meter into the property. That split matters because a leak on your side of the meter is yours to find and fix, and it can run for months before anything shows on the surface.

ACWD says that if your home was built before 1960, the service line is likely galvanized steel. It says these lines tend to leak after 40 to 50 years, and it lists low pressure, a green patch of grass and stains as signs. That is the district's general guidance for its customers, not a finding about any one Decoto street.

When a line does need work, the choice is usually a spot repair or a new line. Our pages on water line repair and repiping explain how that decision is made.

A leak check you can do before you call

These steps come from ACWD's guidance on detecting a water leak. They take little time and tell a plumber a lot.

  1. Test with the house valve

    Turn the house valve off and leave it overnight, then check your usage on the ACWD portal. Usage during the shutoff means the leak is likely outside the house. No usage means look inside.

  2. Dye test the toilets

    Put dye in each tank and wait 20 minutes without flushing. If color shows in the bowl, the flapper is leaking.

  3. Check the irrigation

    ACWD suggests checking sprinklers twice a year, once after winter and once in mid-summer.

  4. Look for an ACWD alert

    ACWD meters can send an email or text if they detect continuous flow. A courtesy notice from the meter reader is the same warning.

Different homes

Shared walls mean shared questions

Detached houses, townhomes, duplexes and condos each answer the question of who owns the pipe differently. If you share walls, find out before a leak starts whether the building has a master shutoff valve and who maintains the shared drain line. A property manager or an owners' association usually holds that information.

When one unit's drain backs up, the cause can be in the shared line instead of the unit. A drain cleaning visit that reaches the shared line is a different job from clearing one tub. Tell us what the neighbors are seeing, because that changes where we start.
