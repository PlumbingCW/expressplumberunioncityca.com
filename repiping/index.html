Repiping in Union City, CA

If you are patching pinhole leaks every few months, or the water has lost pressure and turns brown, the pipes may be finished. Repiping replaces the supply lines so you stop chasing leaks one at a time.

When Repairs Stop Making Sense

A single leak on sound pipe is a repair. A second or third in the same year, on the same type of pipe, is a pattern. Pipes wear from the inside, so the next failure is usually only a matter of time.

Repiping makes sense when leaks keep coming, pressure and flow have dropped, water runs brown after sitting, or the home still has a pipe material with a known failure record. It also makes sense before a remodel opens the walls anyway.

Copper or PEX: What We Would Tell a Friend

Copper

  • Long track record when installed well
  • Rigid, so it needs more joints and fittings
  • Soldered joints are slower to install
  • Higher material cost than PEX
  • Can pinhole in aggressive water or with poor installation

PEX

  • Flexible, fewer fittings and less cutting
  • Faster to install, often less opening in walls
  • Not suited to direct sun exposure
  • Lower material cost than copper
  • Fitting quality and crimp work matter

Neither is wrong. Copper suits owners who want metal in the walls and accept the extra labor. PEX suits owners who want the least disruption. Check your ACWD water quality report, which lists the disinfectant and hardness of your supply, and ask us how it applies to the material you prefer.

Whole-House or Partial?

Partial repipe

One bathroom, a kitchen run or the hot lines only. It suits a localized problem or a remodel, and costs less up front.

Whole-house repipe

Every supply line replaced, including to the water heater. It ends the cycle of leaks and gives uniform pressure.

Trade-off

A partial job leaves old pipe elsewhere, which may fail later. Splicing new pipe to old can also hurt flow if the connected sections differ.

Our approach

We tell you which sections are in worse shape, then you choose how much to do now.

What Happens During a Repipe

  1. Walkthrough

    We look at pipe materials, access, fixtures and the water heater. We note where holes in drywall are likely.

  2. Plan and permit

    We lay out the new routes and plan for the permit and inspection.

  3. Shut off and rough in

    Water is off during the work, and new lines go in through walls, attic or crawlspace.

  4. Test and inspect

    The system is pressure tested and, where required, inspected before any wall is closed.

  5. Patch and restore

    Holes are patched to paint-ready. Finish painting and texture work are usually separate.

Permits and Who May Do the Work

Union City's permit guidance says a plumbing permit is needed for most construction, and that replacing concealed defective pipe is treated as new work needing a permit and inspection. The rule for your specific job can differ, so ask the Building Division at 510.675.5313 before work starts.

California also requires a contractor's license for work valued at 1,000 dollars or more in labor and materials, which most repipes exceed. Check any contractor on the CSLB website or at 800-321-2752.

A whole-house repipe is built to the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), with approved materials, correct support spacing and pressure testing. Ask the contractor how the system will be tested before the walls are closed.

Access, Drywall and Living Through It

Expect small access holes in walls and ceilings, often in closets and utility areas first. Water will be off for part of each working day, so plan for it. We tell you how long the shutoff will run before we start.

Access varies by house. Attic and crawlspace homes are the easiest, while slab homes may need new lines routed through walls or the attic. We also serve Fremont, as described on the Fremont emergency plumber page.
