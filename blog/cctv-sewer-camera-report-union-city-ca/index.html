What a useful report contains

A good report is more than a video. Look for each of these before you rely on it.

Address, date and start point

The report should say where the camera went in, such as a cleanout or a fixture.

Pipe material and size

Cast iron, clay, plastic and bituminized fiber behave differently and fail differently.

A distance counter

Feet from the entry point are shown on the footage and used to log each defect.

A defect log

Each finding should list distance, position on the pipe and a short description.

Photos or stills

Stills of the worst points make it easier to compare against a second opinion.

The conclusion and its basis

A recommendation should point to specific footage, not just a general statement.

Walk the footage in four passes

  1. Check the header

    Confirm the address, date, start point and that the distance counter starts at zero.

  2. Follow the counter to the main

    Does the footage reach the connection to the main, or stop short? A stopped inspection is an incomplete answer.

  3. Match each defect to the log

    Pause at each logged distance and see whether the footage supports the description.

  4. Compare the summary to what you saw

    If the summary calls the pipe failing and the footage shows a few light scale marks, ask why.

Ask whether the line was cleaned before the camera went in, and see sewer line repair for the options once you know what the defects are. Heavy grease or debris can hide defects, and a pre-cleaned pipe shows more.

Monitor or act

Often fine to monitor

  • Light scale on cast iron with good flow
  • Isolated grease or debris that clears with cleaning
  • A single hairline crack with no roots or soil
  • A shallow sag with no standing water

Usually worth acting on

  • Roots at several joints
  • A fracture, collapse or missing pipe wall
  • A deep belly that holds water
  • Displaced joints with soil visible
  • An obstruction the camera cannot pass

Notes alone do not decide. The footage, the pipe material and the age of the house all go into the call.

What the footage needs to do for Union Sanitary District

In Union City, the owner owns the whole lateral to USD's main. USD allows an old capped lateral to be reused only if it is connected, in good condition, has video submitted and meets current standards. If you plan to reuse a lateral on a rebuild, ask the inspector to give you a complete recording you can hand over.

USD also requires a repair permit before any lateral work, and it asks you to notify your USD inspector 48 hours before an inspection. Ask the contractor to keep the camera file and stills in a form you can share with USD or a second inspector.

Getting a better report in the first place

Ask the inspector to clean the line first if it is slow or backing up, since debris hides defects. Ask for the full recording, not only clips, and for the distance counter to stay on screen.

Tell the inspector the house age, any past backups and any cleanout you know about. That context helps the operator read what the camera sees, and it gives you a record to compare with later.

When to get a second opinion

  • ✓The report calls for full replacement but the footage shows a single defect
  • ✓The summary and the footage do not match
  • ✓There is no distance counter or the inspection stopped before the main
  • ✓You were not given a copy of the video
  • ✓The only option offered is the most expensive one, with no spot repair or lining comparison
  • ✓The inspector is also the only one pitching the repair and a deadline is pushed
  • ✓You are buying a house and the repair estimate would change your offer

What a camera cannot tell you

A camera shows the inside of the pipe. It does not show soil conditions, how the pipe was bedded or whether a stoppage will come back next week. It also sees only what the operator points it at, so a short run can miss a defect further down.

Treat the report as strong evidence, not a verdict. Pair it with the pipe age, the house history and what the drains actually do.
