Toilet Keeps Clogging in Union City, CA

One clog is an accident. A toilet that keeps clogging has a cause. Here is how to read the pattern and decide what to do.

Try this first, in ten minutes

Most clogs in a single toilet clear with the right tool and a little patience. Work through these in order.

  • ✓Stop flushing. If the bowl is rising, lift the tank lid and push the flapper down, or shut the valve behind the toilet.
  • ✓Use a flange plunger. The flange fits into the drain. Seal it, push gently, then pull sharply, and repeat.
  • ✓Add warm water and dish soap. Use hot tap water, not boiling, which can crack porcelain. Let the soap sit for a few minutes.
  • ✓Try a closet auger. Feed it into the bowl, crank and withdraw. The sleeve protects the porcelain.
  • ✓Skip chemical drain openers. They rarely clear toilets, can sit in the bowl and are hazardous to the next person who works on it.

If the toilet drains but the water goes down slowly every time, the problem may not be a clog at all. See the table below.

Hard water and the sewer lateral in Union City

ACWD reports blended hardness for Union City at 120 to 160 ppm, averaging 138 ppm. Over years that mineral scale can crust the small rim jets under the bowl lip, which weakens the flush. If your flush sounds weak, a vinegar soak of the rim holes is a cheap thing to try.

If several drains are slow, the problem may be the lateral. Call Union Sanitary District at (510) 477-7500, answered 24 hours a day. USD inspects the public main at no charge, and if the main is clear the blockage is probably in your lateral, which you own.

Toilet and drain installation follows the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), which sets venting and drain sizing. A toilet that clogs again and again after a clean snaking can point to a venting or slope problem, which is a code question, not just a clog.

Fix it yourself or call a plumber

Tools and fixes worth trying

  • A flange plunger, not a cup plunger
  • A closet auger with a rubber sleeve
  • Warm water with dish soap
  • A vinegar soak for clogged rim jets
  • Stopping non-flushable items for a few weeks to see if the pattern changes

Signs to stop and call

  • A second clog in the same toilet within weeks
  • The auger comes back clean but the toilet still drains slowly
  • Water rises in the tub or shower when the toilet flushes
  • Gurgling in other drains or sewage smell
  • Sewage coming up from a floor drain

A repeat clog is often a signal, not bad luck. If the lateral is the cause, see drain cleaning or a camera inspection rather than plunging endlessly.

Plunger isn't working and the water is rising?

Stop flushing and shut off the valve behind the toilet. If the bowl keeps filling or sewage is backing up, call the line.

Causes inside the toilet itself

Too much paper or wipes

Wipes labeled flushable often do not break down like toilet paper, and they catch in the trap.

Mineral buildup in the rim jets

Scale narrows the jets that start the siphon, so the bowl empties slowly.

Old high-volume toilet design

Older toilets sometimes clog less on paper but waste more water. Some early low-flow models had weak flushes.

Failing fill valve or flapper

Low water in the tank means a weak flush and a bowl that never fully clears.

Objects dropped in

Toys, brushes and hygiene products can lodge in the trap and need a plumber to pull the toilet.

A loose or damaged flange

A rocking toilet or a leak at the base can come with repeated slow draining.

Habits that stop repeat clogs

Use a modest amount of toilet paper and flush twice if you need more. Keep wipes, cotton products and paper towels out of the bowl, whatever the label says. A small trash can near the toilet gets used when it is within reach.

If kids use the bathroom, a quick check for toys before a flush saves a service call. These habits matter more than any gadget.

Replacing the toilet is sometimes the real fix

California's 2025 green building code sets toilets at 1.28 gallons per flush or less. Modern 1.28 gpf models are designed to clear waste on that volume, and a good one often outperforms an older toilet that has scaled up inside. If yours clogs often and is a weak early low-flow design, replacing it is a fair choice.

Before you buy, check the flush type, the rough-in distance from the wall and whether the new bowl matches the flange height. A plumber can also check the flange and wax ring when the old toilet is up, which is when hidden leaks and loose flanges are easiest to find.
