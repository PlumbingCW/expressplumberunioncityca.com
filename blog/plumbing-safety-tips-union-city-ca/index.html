Plumbing Safety Tips in Union City, CA

Most plumbing damage is made worse in the first ten minutes. Knowing where the shutoffs are and what not to touch matters more than any tool.

Know These Five Spots Before You Need Them

1

Main water shutoff

Usually where the water line enters the house, or near the meter. Test it once a year so it turns.

2

Fixture stop valves

Under sinks and behind toilets. These let you isolate one fixture without losing water to the house.

3

Water heater shutoff

The cold supply valve above the heater, and the gas control or breaker.

4

Gas shutoff

Know where the meter valve is and that only a utility or qualified person should reopen it.

5

Sewer cleanout

Where the drain line can be accessed outside. Knowing it saves time in a backup.

If a Pipe Is Spraying or Flooding

  1. Shut off the water

    Turn a round gate valve clockwise until it stops, or turn a lever handle a quarter turn. Open a low faucet to drain pressure.

  2. Keep clear of electricity

    If water is near outlets or the panel, stay clear and never touch a breaker while standing in water. Tell the plumber or an electrician about it.

  3. Contain and photograph

    Move belongings, mop what you can and take photos for any insurance claim.

  4. Call a plumber

    Once the water is off, the problem is stable. Call the 24/7 line and describe what you see.

Local Numbers and Rules Worth Saving

If you smell gas, leave the building, avoid anything that could spark, and call 911. Then call PG&E at 1-800-743-5000. Do not stay to look for the source or try to repair a gas leak yourself.

ACWD is responsible for pipes on the street side of the water meter, and you own the pipe from the meter inward. A leak at or beyond the meter on your side is yours to fix. For a leak on the street side, ACWD Operations is at (510) 668-6500.

California requires water heaters to be braced or strapped against earthquake motion (Health and Safety Code 19211), and the 1868 Hayward Fault earthquake ruptured from Warm Springs in Fremont to San Leandro. Look at your heater now. If the straps are loose or missing, that is a quick fix worth making.

Before any digging, call 811 at least two working days ahead. It is free, and in California it is the law.

The safety steps above are habits, not a substitute for code. Fixed plumbing in California follows the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), so work such as relocating a gas line, adding a shutoff or changing a water heater should be done and inspected to that standard, not improvised.

A Quick Home Safety Walk

  • ✓Test your main shutoff by closing and reopening it gently.
  • ✓Look under every sink for drips, rust or swollen cabinet floors.
  • ✓Check the water heater for rust at the base, scorching and loose straps.
  • ✓Know the smell of gas and keep the utility numbers on your phone.
  • ✓Check flexible hoses on washers and toilets for bulges or cracks.
  • ✓Keep grease and wipes out of the drain, even labeled flushable ones.

Do this walk twice a year, perhaps when you change smoke alarm batteries. Ten minutes is enough, and it is how most slow leaks get caught before they stain a ceiling.

Water Heater and Drain Safety

A water heater is the one appliance in the house that stores a large volume of hot water under pressure. Keep the area around it clear, look for rust at the base, and never block or cap the relief valve discharge pipe. A setting around 120 degrees Fahrenheit is commonly recommended to limit scalding.

Hoses deserve a mention too. Rubber washing machine hoses harden and split with age, so replace them with braided lines every few years and turn the washer valves off when you leave for a trip. A burst hose can put out a lot of water in an hour.

Chemical drain cleaners are another hazard. They can burn skin and eyes, damage older pipes and leave a plumber working in caustic water later. If a plunger does not clear it, stop and call.

Safe DIY or Call a Plumber

Reasonable DIY

  • Closing fixture stops and the main valve
  • Replacing a faucet aerator or a toilet flapper
  • Clearing a sink with a plunger
  • Checking water heater straps

Call a plumber

  • Any gas smell or gas line work, after calling 911 and PG&E
  • A leak that continues after the shutoff is closed
  • Water heater relief valve discharging repeatedly
  • Discolored water, sewer smells or backups
