Sewer Lateral Responsibility in Union City, CA

Many owners assume the sewer line under the street is the district's problem. It is not. This guide explains who owns each piece, what Union Sanitary District requires and who to call first.

The short answer

In Union City, the property owner is responsible for the entire sewer lateral, from the house to where it connects to Union Sanitary District's main sewer line. That includes the stretch under your sidewalk and under the street.

Union Sanitary District (USD) provides sewer service to Union City, Fremont and Newark. The main in the street belongs to USD. The pipe that connects your house to that main belongs to you.

Where your lateral starts and ends

The lateral begins where the house drain leaves the building and runs through your yard, under the sidewalk and under the street to the connection on the main. Along the way it may have a cleanout, a straight access point for clearing and camera work. Many owners have never seen theirs.

Knowing the route matters because every problem along it is yours to fix, from roots in a joint to a crushed section under a driveway. A sewer line repair scope should name each stretch it covers, not just the spot where the blockage showed up.

Four mistakes owners make

Assuming the district fixes the street portion

The part under the street still belongs to the owner up to the main connection.

Assuming a clear main means no problem

If USD finds the main clear, the blockage is probably in your lateral.

Skipping the permit on private property

USD requires a repair permit before any lateral work, even on private property.

Opening a manhole to look

Only USD personnel are authorized to open and inspect those structures.

When sewage backs up, work in this order

  1. Stop using water

    Do not flush or run sinks and showers. More water only pushes more sewage into the lowest fixture.

  2. Check for a cleanout

    Look just outside the building or just behind the sidewalk. Do not pour chemicals in.

  3. Call USD at (510) 477-7500

    The line is answered 24 hours a day. USD inspects the public main at no charge.

  4. Read the result

    If the main is clear, the blockage is probably in your lateral and the cost sits with you.

  5. Call a plumber

    A camera inspection shows what is wrong, and the permit comes before any repair.

Keeping the next backup from happening

Most lateral backups come from roots, grease and things that should not be flushed. Roots enter at joints and cracks, so an older clay or cast iron lateral is the usual suspect. Grease sticks to pipe walls in the kitchen line and builds until water stops moving.

A camera pass through the lateral shows how much life the pipe has left, and a sewer inspection gives you something to plan around. Keep the cleanout reachable too, since a buried one costs time when you need it most.

What USD and the city require before work starts

USD requires a repair permit before any lateral work, even repairs on private property. Digging in the street or right-of-way also needs a city encroachment permit, which you can ask about at (510) 675-5305. Ask your plumber who is pulling each permit before the crew shows up.

Call 811 at least 2 working days before anyone digs. The call is free and California law requires it. USD also asks that you notify your USD inspector 48 hours before an inspection, so work scheduling should account for both lead times.

Questions to ask a contractor before a lateral repair

  • ✓Who pulls the USD permit? The permit is required before work begins and should be in hand, not promised.
  • ✓Is there camera footage of the whole lateral? Ask to see it and keep a copy, before and after.
  • ✓Does the scope include the section under the sidewalk and street? That part is yours, and it often needs the encroachment permit.
  • ✓Who calls 811? It should happen at least 2 working days before digging.
  • ✓Is the contractor licensed for the job? California requires a license for work valued at one thousand dollars or more, and you can verify at the CSLB.
