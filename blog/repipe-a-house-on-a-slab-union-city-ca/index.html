Repipe a House on a Slab in Union City, CA

Why slab houses are repiped differently

In a house with a crawlspace, a plumber can crawl under and replace lines in the open. On a slab, many of the water lines run in or under the concrete. Reaching a failing line there means breaking the floor.

That is why most slab repipes abandon the old lines and run new ones overhead or inside walls, where they are easy to reach. The old pipe stays in place, capped and unused. The result is plumbing you can service later without a jackhammer.

Signs the pipes are reaching the end

Repeat pinhole leaks

A second or third leak in a few years usually means the pipe wall is thinning everywhere, not just at one spot.

A warm patch on the floor

Warmth under tile or carpet can mean a hot line leaking beneath the slab.

A meter that moves when nobody is home

Turn off every fixture and watch the meter. Then close the house valve and check again, as ACWD suggests. Movement with the valve closed means the leak is outside the house plumbing, and no movement points inside.

Rusty or metallic first draw

Brown water that clears after a minute points to corroding steel or old fittings.

Falling pressure that keeps spreading

Galvanized pipe fills from the inside with rust, so pressure drops at more fixtures over time.

Water running sounds at night

A hiss or trickle when every fixture is off is worth chasing with listening equipment.

What a slab-house repipe involves

  1. Map the lines

    The plumber finds the main shutoff, traces which fixtures feed from which branch and notes what is under the slab.

  2. Choose the route

    New lines go through the attic or inside walls. The route depends on the floor plan and what is above the ceiling.

  3. Open small access points

    Drywall is cut near fixtures and along the new path. Ask who patches and paints, because scopes differ.

  4. Run and connect the new lines

    PEX or copper goes in, fixtures are reconnected and the old slab lines are capped.

  5. Pressure test and restore

    The system is pressurized and checked with a gauge for drops before walls are closed.

Pipe age and ownership in Union City

The Alameda County Water District says homes built before 1960 likely have a galvanized steel service line, which tends to leak after 40 to 50 years. Warning signs include low pressure, a green patch in the lawn and stains. ACWD owns the pipe on the street side of the meter, and you own everything from the meter inward, so ask any bidder whether the new plumbing starts at the meter or at the house wall.

The city's published guidance says a permit is needed for most construction, and replacing concealed defective pipe counts as new work needing a permit and inspection. It does not name repipes, so call the Building Division at 510.675.5313 before you sign and ask who pulls the permit.

New supply piping has to meet the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), including approved pipe materials, fittings and pressure testing. Ask a contractor to name the pipe material and how the system will be tested, because that answer shows whether the quote follows code.

What moves the price of a slab repipe

Fixtures and bathroomsEvery fixture, hose bib and appliance line is a run of pipe. More of them means more material and labor.
One story or twoAttic access and ceiling height change how long each run takes.
PEX or copperThe material changes both parts cost and installation time.
Drywall openings and finishThe number of holes, and whether the contractor patches and paints, changes the scope.
Permit, inspection and fixturesPermit fees, shutoffs, pressure regulators and a water heater hookup update can be separate items.
A low bid that leaves out drywall, permit or the meter-to-house section is not low, it is incomplete.

Before you sign a repipe contract

  • ✓A written scope that lists every line replaced, including hose bibs and the ice maker line
  • ✓The material named, and whether any fittings are left from the old system
  • ✓Where the new plumbing connects, at the meter side or inside the house
  • ✓Who pulls the permit and who schedules the inspection
  • ✓Who patches drywall and who paints
  • ✓A plan for the days the water will be off
  • ✓A contractor license that checks out with the CSLB, which California requires for work valued at one thousand dollars or more
