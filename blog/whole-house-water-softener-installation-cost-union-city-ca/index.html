Cost and need, answered first

A whole house softener has no single price. The installed cost depends on the unit, the plumbing around it and the drain it needs, and a fair quote lists each of those on its own line. Before you compare totals, decide whether your house needs a softener at all.

Union City water is hard, but it is not extreme. For many homes a softener is a comfort and appliance-life choice, not a rescue. If your main complaint is chlorine taste or smell, a softener will not fix it.

What ACWD says is in Union City tap water

The Alameda County Water District delivers blended water to Union City, a mix of purchased San Francisco water and local groundwater. The 2025 ACWD water quality report lists that blend at 120 to 160 ppm hardness as calcium carbonate, with an average of 138 ppm. ACWD says to divide by 17.1 for grains per gallon, which puts the average near 8 gpg.

The same report names chloramines as the disinfectant, and a salt softener does not remove them. Use the 138 ppm average to size a unit, and check ACWD's hardness map for your own neighborhood so every contractor quotes from the same number.

A softener install should follow the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), including a proper drain connection with an air gap and a bypass valve. Ask the installer where the brine line will drain and how the bypass works before you compare quotes.

Source type: Alameda County Water District 2025 Water Quality Report

Salt-based or salt-free: an honest comparison

Salt-based (ion exchange) softener

  • Removes the calcium and magnesium that cause hardness, so scale stops forming
  • Needs salt refills and a drain line for its regeneration cycle
  • Adds sodium in proportion to the hardness it removes
  • Uses some water every time it regenerates
  • Best fit if you want soap that lathers and glass that dries spot-free

Salt-free (scale conditioner)

  • Does not remove hardness, it changes how minerals crystallize so they cling less
  • No salt, no drain and very little upkeep
  • Water still tests hard and feels the same on skin
  • Results vary by water, so treat big claims with caution
  • Best fit if you only want less scale and cannot plumb a drain

Neither type removes chloramines. If taste is the goal, a carbon filter rated for chloramine is the right tool, and it can sit alongside either softener.

What drives the installed cost

Capacity and valve typeUnits are sized by household size and hardness. A metered valve regenerates only when needed, and that choice changes both the price and the salt you use.
Location and plumbingA garage with a main line at hand is simpler than a side yard that needs new piping, shutoffs and a bypass. Copper or PEX, and the distance to run, add parts and labor. This is ordinary plumbing installation work, so the quote should price it like any other pipe run.
Drain and powerA salt-based unit needs a drain with a proper air gap and an outlet nearby. Long drain runs or a missing outlet add work.
Filters and add-onsA sediment filter or chloramine carbon tank ahead of the softener means more hardware and more fittings.
Permit and accessNew piping can bring a permit question. The city Building Division can tell you whether your layout needs one.
Compare quotes line by line, not by total. Two totals that look alike can hide different capacities, valve types and drain work.

Signs you can skip the softener

A softener is a fair purchase, but not every household needs one. You can probably hold off if most of these fit.

  • ✓Your complaint is taste or smell. That points to chloramines and a filter, not hardness.
  • ✓The scale is cosmetic. A little spotting on glass or a faucet crust that a vinegar soak clears may not justify a new system.
  • ✓You may move soon. A renter or short-term owner rarely recovers the cost.
  • ✓You want zero upkeep and have no drain. A salt-free conditioner or a point-of-use filter is a reasonable middle path.
  • ✓You limit sodium. Ask for a bypass so the kitchen cold tap stays unsoftened, or skip the softener.

On the other side, a softener earns its place when you see white crust on showerheads, spotty dishes, scale in the water heater and soap that never rinses clean. Those are signs the hardness is costing you.

How to get softener quotes you can compare

  1. Settle on one hardness number

    Use the ACWD figure for your neighborhood or a test strip result, and give the same number to every contractor.

  2. Ask for capacity in grains and the valve type

    A quote that says only 'whole house softener' hides the most important spec.

  3. Ask where the drain goes

    Look for an air gap, a drain location and who handles any trenching or wall work.

  4. Ask about the bypass and kitchen tap

    A bypass lets you service the unit without shutting the house down, and a kitchen cold line can stay unsoftened.

  5. Ask about salt, upkeep and the permit question

    Find out how often salt is added, what a service visit includes and whether the install needs city sign-off.

Quotes that answer all five are comparable. Quotes that skip them are guesses.
