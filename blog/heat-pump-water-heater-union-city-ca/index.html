Heat Pump Water Heater in Union City, CA

The Date You Keep Hearing About

The Bay Area Air Quality Management District adopted zero-NOx water heater rules (Rule 9-6) on March 15, 2023. The date for natural gas water heaters is currently scheduled for January 2027, and it applies when you replace a burned-out unit. A proposed one-year delay, plus new exemptions, is under Board consideration.

Those draft changes were released in 2026, and the Air District Board is expected to consider them this year. Nothing is final until the Board acts, so check the Air District's Rule 9-6 page for the outcome before you plan around either date.

Incentives and Your Electric Provider

The proposed Rule 9-6 changes would add exemptions for low-income owners, for homes with space or ventilation limits, and for electrical limits such as a panel below 100 amps. These are drafts, not adopted rules. If your home might qualify, ask your plumber to note the limit in writing, because the final language may differ.

BayREN offers a 1,000 dollar incentive, paid to a participating contractor, for a qualifying heat pump water heater that replaces a gas or propane unit. The heater must have a UEF of 3.3 or higher and be on the Qualified Products List, and the customer must be with Ava Community Energy. Union City is served by Ava, and customers can return to PG&E at any time, so confirm your account before you buy.

Ask any contractor you consider whether they participate in the program. Do not assume the incentive appears on the quote.

Water heater work in California is governed by the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and a heat pump unit also needs a suitable electrical circuit. Ask any installer which code sections apply to your layout before you approve the plan, so the permit and inspection are not a surprise.

Gas Tank and Heat Pump Tank, Side by Side

Gas storage tank

  • Fast recovery and a familiar install
  • Needs a gas line and a vent to the outside
  • Lower equipment cost at the supply house
  • Covered by the gas rule dates above

Heat pump water heater

  • Pulls heat from surrounding air, so it needs open space
  • Needs an electrical circuit, often 240 volts
  • Needs a condensate drain and clearance on all sides
  • Runs on electricity and puts no combustion gases in the house

Neither choice is wrong. The right one depends on your garage or closet, your panel and your household.

Four Things That Decide Whether It Fits

Air and space

A heat pump heater cools the room it sits in. A cramped closet with no air exchange is a poor home for it, and a garage is often a good one.

Electrical panel

A dedicated circuit and available panel capacity are required. An older or full panel can add an electrician's work to the quote.

Condensate and noise

The unit produces water that must drain somewhere, and it hums like a small appliance. Plan the drain and the room next to it.

Hot water habits

Heat pump mode recovers more slowly than gas. Large households may want a bigger tank or hybrid mode.

Old gas heater showing its age?

Describe the unit and where it sits. A plumber can tell you what a heat pump swap would involve in your house.

How to Plan a Heat Pump Swap

  1. Check the dates

    Look at the Air District's Rule 9-6 page for the latest status on the January 2027 date and the proposed delay.

  2. Check the panel and space

    Have your plumber or electrician confirm circuit capacity, clearances and condensate routing before you buy.

  3. Confirm incentive eligibility

    Verify your Ava account, the UEF rating and Qualified Products List status, and ask whether the contractor participates.

  4. Ask for the written scope

    The quote should list the heater, circuit, drain, strapping, permit and disposal. Our water heater replacement page and water heater installation page show what a complete job includes.
