What builds the price of a tank replacement

The tank itself is one line on the quote. The rest depends on what has to change around it.

Capacity and fuelA 40-gallon gas unit and a 50-gallon electric unit are different products. Size matches your household, and fuel matches what is already connected.
Efficiency ratingHigher efficiency models cost more up front and use less energy over time. Ask for the UEF number on each model quoted.
Venting and gas workA gas heater needs a vent that meets code and a gas connection in good shape. Corroded venting or an old connector adds work.
Code itemsSeismic bracing, an expansion tank, a drain pan with a drain line and a proper discharge pipe are normal parts of a code-compliant install.
Location and accessA garage closet is easier than an attic or a tight alcove. Stairs, tight turns and long pipe runs add labor.
Permit and haul-awayPermit fees and removal of the old tank are sometimes separate lines and sometimes rolled in.
Ask each contractor to list these items separately. A total with no breakdown tells you nothing about what you are getting.

If the heater fails at a bad time

Shut the cold water supply valve on top of the unit, and turn off power at the breaker or set a gas heater to pilot or off. If water is leaking, the floor and the surroundings matter more than the tank, so move anything valuable away from the puddle.

If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 first, then PG&E at 1-800-743-5000. Do not flip switches or use a phone indoors until you are out.

Heat pump units need clearance and airflow, so the closet or garage layout matters as much as the price tag.

Repair or replace the tank?

Repair usually makes sense when

  • The heater is leaking from a fitting, valve or connection, not from the tank
  • A thermostat, element or gas valve has failed on a unit that is otherwise sound
  • The data plate shows the unit is well within its expected life, which is often around a decade for tank heaters
  • The unit is still under the manufacturer's coverage

Replacement usually makes sense when

  • Water is leaking from the tank body or from the base
  • Hot water runs rusty or the tank sediment will not flush out
  • You have paid for repeated repairs in the last couple of years
  • The unit is at or past a typical service life

A leak at the bottom of the tank is the most common reason for a replacement. If a repair is the better answer, water heater repair often costs far less than a new tank.

Rules that touch a Union City tank replacement

California requires new and replacement water heaters to be braced, anchored or strapped against earthquake movement. A quote that leaves out bracing is leaving out a code item, so look for it as a line.

Bay Area gas heaters face a zero-NOx rule. The Air District's Rule 9-6 is currently scheduled for January 2027 and applies when you replace a burned-out unit. A one-year delay is proposed and the Air District Board is expected to consider it this year. Ask each contractor what they can install on the date you need it.

BayREN also lists a 1,000 dollar incentive paid to participating contractors for qualifying heat pump water heaters replacing gas, for Ava Community Energy customers, so ask whether it is on your quote.

Source type: California Health and Safety Code 19211 and Bay Area Air Quality Management District Rule 9-6

How to compare two water heater quotes

  1. Photograph the data plate

    Capacity, fuel, UEF and age give every contractor the same starting point.

  2. Match capacity, fuel and warranty term

    Compare like with like. A different tank size or a shorter warranty makes a cheaper number meaningless.

  3. Look for the code items

    Bracing, expansion tank, pan with drain, discharge pipe and shutoffs should each appear.

  4. Find the permit and haul-away lines

    If they are missing, ask whether they are included or extra.

  5. Ask what happens if the vent or gas line needs work

    A good quote says how a surprise will be handled before it happens.

Get at least two quotes, and give each contractor the same photo of the data plate and the same description of the location. If one number is far lower, the missing lines are usually the reason. If it is far higher, ask what extra work is in it and why.
