Spot Repair, Lining or Full Replacement

Repair is a decision between three paths. The right one depends on how much of the pipe is bad, what it is made of and what sits above it.

Lining or spot repair fits when

  • Damage is limited to one section or a few joints
  • The pipe is otherwise straight and holds its grade
  • Digging would tear up a driveway, patio or mature landscaping
  • Roots are entering at joints and the rest of the pipe is sound
  • The camera shows no major bellies or collapses

Full replacement fits when

  • The pipe has multiple breaks, offsets or long cracked runs
  • A belly holds standing water and solids
  • The pipe is collapsed, crushed or heavily corroded
  • The material is failing, such as old fiber or deteriorated cast iron
  • A lined pipe would leave the bore too small to serve the house

We do not push replacement when a smaller repair will do, and we say so when a repair would not last. A sound pipe with one bad joint should not be dug up end to end.

Two common methods

Cast Iron Pipe Repair and Trenchless Pipe Lining

Cast iron pipe repair

Cast iron was common for house drains and rusts from the inside. Scale builds up and narrows the bore, and the wall can thin until it cracks. Cracks and heavily scaled sections can be cut out and replaced with plastic pipe using no-hub couplings, or the whole run can be replaced. We look at the pipe by camera and by touch before recommending.

Trenchless pipe lining

A resin-soaked liner is pulled through the old pipe and cured in place, forming a new pipe inside the old one. It avoids trenching, so lawns and driveways stay put. It needs a pipe that is still in a straight, continuous path, and the pipe must be cleaned and checked before lining.

Pipe bursting

A bursting head pulls new pipe through the old path and breaks the old one apart. It works when the old pipe is too damaged to line. It needs entry and exit pits.

Open trench replacement

Digging remains the standard for pipes with bellies or heavy damage, since the grade can be reset. It is more disruptive, and it is the option most affected by what is on top of the pipe.

Permits, 811 and Ownership in Union City

In Union City, the owner owns the whole sewer lateral to the USD main, including under the sidewalk and street. A USD repair permit is required before any lateral work, even on private property, and digging in the street also needs a city encroachment permit. Call 811 at least two working days before any excavation, since it is free and required in California.

So the repair date depends on permit steps as much as on the crew, and the practical move is to start them early. Replacing a concealed defective pipe counts as new work needing a city permit and inspection, so ask the Building Division at 510.675.5313 how that applies to your job.

From Diagnosis to a Finished Repair

  1. Confirm the damage

    A camera run locates the break, shows the pipe material and measures how far it extends. Keep the footage, since it helps when you talk to USD or another contractor.

  2. Choose the repair

    We lay out spot repair, lining and replacement for your situation, with the trade-offs in plain terms.

  3. Permit and utility locate

    A USD repair permit goes in before work starts, and 811 marks the buried utilities. Street work also involves the city.

  4. Do the work

    Excavate, line or replace. We keep the line out of service as briefly as the job allows and tell you what water use to avoid in the meantime.

  5. Inspect and verify

    USD asks for notice 48 hours before inspection. After the inspection, a camera pass confirms the finished line.
