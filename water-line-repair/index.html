Whose Pipe Is It? Start at the Meter

Water bubbling up in the yard or the street is urgent. Call the 24/7 line, close your main house valve (ACWD says clockwise), and take photos of the wet area. Same-day response is the aim for an active leak that is flooding a yard or undermining a driveway.

The water meter marks the boundary. ACWD is responsible for the pipe on the street side. You are responsible from the meter inward, which includes the line to the house and any shutoffs on your side.

Galvanized Lines in Older Homes

ACWD says that if your home was built before 1960, your service line is likely galvanized steel, and galvanized lines tend to leak after 40 to 50 years. Signs include low pressure, a patch of unusually green grass, and stains. If your home fits that age, a leak is not a surprise, and the question becomes whether to patch or replace the whole line.

Source type: ACWD galvanized service line leak page

Signs the Service Line Is Failing

Low pressure at every tap

Corrosion buildup inside galvanized steel narrows the bore until flow drops.

Green or soggy patch

A strip of lush grass or wet ground between the meter and the house, with no sprinkler running.

Meter turns with all fixtures off

Run the overnight shut-off test, covered on our leak detection page.

Rusty or discolored water

Brown water after a period of no use points to steel rusting inside.

Sinkholes or sunken pavement

Water that escapes under concrete can wash out the soil beneath it.

Sound of water in the yard

A hiss near the meter or along the pipe path when everything is off.

Trenching or Trenchless Replacement

Both put a new line in the ground. The difference is how much of your yard gets opened.

Open trench

  • Direct access to the old line
  • Best for short runs or shallow lines
  • Reliable and widely used
  • Lawn, paving or landscaping must be restored

Trenchless

  • Small entry and exit pits only
  • Keeps driveways and gardens mostly intact
  • Not suited to every soil or layout
  • Cost depends on length, depth and obstacles

We tell you which fits your yard after we see where the line runs. Dig only after calling 811. It is free, legally required in California, and needs at least two working days' notice.

Repair or Replace: How We Decide

  1. Locate and expose

    We trace the line, mark it, and open the smallest area that reaches the leak.

  2. Inspect the pipe

    If the pipe around the leak is thin, pitted or choked with rust, we show you.

  3. Weigh the options

    A fitting failure on sound pipe can be repaired. A corroded line is usually worth replacing end to end.

  4. Plan the work

    Depth, length, utilities, trees and surface all affect the choice between a trench and trenchless.

  5. Permits and pressure test

    Replacing concealed pipe is new work under the Union City permit rules, so we plan for it, and we test the new line before backfilling.

Backflow Testing and the Annual Test

A backflow preventer keeps water from your property from flowing back into the public supply. ACWD says an installed device must be tested by a certified tester at least once a year, and the district may ask a customer to install a device at the meter after a survey.

ACWD keeps a list of approved local testers. We can test, repair or replace an assembly, and you can reach the ACWD backflow office at 510-668-6584 with questions about your account.
