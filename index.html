Signs you need an emergency plumber now

Some plumbing problems can wait until Monday. These cannot, because the damage grows every hour.

Water that will not stop

A pipe that keeps running after you close the valve means you need the main shutoff. Turn the house valve clockwise, then see burst pipe repair for what happens next.

Sewage coming up

Several drains gurgling at once, or sewage in a tub or floor drain, points to the main line. Stop using water and read about sewer line repair while you wait.

A leaking or dead water heater

Water pooling at the base of a tank is a failing tank, not a loose fitting. Cold showers with no leak often mean a part failure, which water heater repair can fix.

Wet walls or a spinning meter

A stain spreading on a ceiling or a meter that moves with every tap closed means a hidden leak. Leak detection finds it with acoustic gear and thermal imaging before walls are opened.

A toilet that keeps rising

Close the stop valve behind the toilet before the bowl overflows. If the water still climbs, the blockage is deeper than the trap and a drain machine is needed.

A gas smell

Gas is not a plumber-first problem. Leave the building, call 911, then call PG&E at 1-800-743-5000 from outside.

What to do in the first ten minutes of a leak

The first few minutes decide whether you are mopping a floor or replacing one. Work through these in order.

  1. Stop the water

    Close the fixture stop valve if the leak is at a sink or toilet. If you cannot find it, turn the main house valve clockwise, which is the shutoff Alameda County Water District points owners to.

  2. Take the heater out of the picture

    If a tank heater is leaking, switch off the electric breaker or turn the gas control to off. Do not relight anything until the tank is dry and inspected.

  3. Contain and photograph

    Move rugs and electronics, put towels or a bucket where water drips, and take photos of the damage. Photos help later if you file an insurance claim.

  4. Call and describe it

    Tell the dispatcher where the water is, what you shut off and whether it has stopped. That detail lets us line up the right plumber and tools.

Union City water and sewer: what it means for your repair

Union City's tap water is a blend of purchased San Francisco supply and local groundwater, and the blended hardness runs from 120 to 160 ppm with an average of 138 ppm, about 8 grains per gallon. That is enough to leave scale on fixtures and sediment in a tank heater, so a yearly tank flush is worth asking about, and the water softener and filtration page covers treatment options.

Sewer service comes from Union Sanitary District, and USD says the owner is responsible for the entire lateral from the house to the main, including the part under the sidewalk and street. Before paying for excavation, ask USD to check the public main, because it does that free of charge. Any lateral repair needs a USD permit, even on private property.

ACWD says homes built before 1960 are likely to have galvanized steel service lines, which tend to leak after 40 to 50 years. If your house predates 1960 and your pressure has dropped, say so on the call.

Repair or replace: a quick way to decide

A good plumber will tell you when a repair is not worth the money. These rules of thumb apply to heaters, fixtures and pipes alike.

Repair usually makes sense when

  • One part has failed, such as a thermostat, heating element, fill valve or cartridge.
  • The leak is at a fitting or connection and the tank or pipe body is sound.
  • A clog is a one-time event and the camera shows a healthy line.
  • The repair costs far less than a new unit and the old one is not near the end of its life.

Replacement usually makes sense when

  • A tank is leaking from the body, because a rusted-through tank cannot be patched.
  • The same appliance has needed several repairs in a short time.
  • Pinhole leaks appear in more than one spot, which points to pipe wall failure.
  • A camera shows a cracked, offset or root-filled sewer pipe that keeps clogging.
