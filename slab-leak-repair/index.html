Water Under the Slab Is an Urgent Problem

Call the 24/7 line if the floor is hot underfoot, the meter runs with everything off, or you can hear water that nobody is using. Close the main house valve (ACWD says clockwise) and turn the water heater to pilot or off so it does not heat an empty tank.

Same-day response matters here because a pressurized leak under concrete can wash out the soil beneath the slab and soak flooring, baseboards and drywall from below. The sooner the water is off, the smaller the repair.

How to Tell a Slab Leak From Other Leaks

Slab leaks are easy to confuse with a toilet or irrigation leak. These clues narrow it down.

  • ✓Hot spots. A patch of floor warmer than the rest, often along the path of a hot line.
  • ✓Hissing at night. Pressurized water makes a faint sound that carries through concrete when the house is quiet.
  • ✓Bill climbs, no visible leak. The meter turns with all fixtures off and the overnight shut-off test shows a loss.
  • ✓Damp carpet or lifting flooring. Moisture rising from below shows at edges, closets and doorways first.
  • ✓Low hot water pressure. A hot line leaking under the floor can weaken flow at fixtures further along.
  • ✓Cracks or a wet foundation edge. Water pushing outward sometimes shows at the slab perimeter.

Why Slab Leaks Show Up in Homes of This Era

Many homes built from the 1950s through the 1980s sit on concrete slabs with supply lines running in or under the concrete. Copper was the usual material. Over decades, water chemistry, rubbing against concrete and gravel, and small movements in the slab can wear a pinhole through the wall of the tube.

We cannot tell you from the street whether your home has this layout, but your plans, a walk-through or a quick look at where the water line enters the house will show it. A home with supply lines in the attic or walls has a different set of leak risks.

How a Slab Leak Gets Found and Fixed

  1. Confirm the leak is on the pressurized side

    We close fixtures, watch the meter, and isolate hot from cold to see which line is losing water.

  2. Locate it from above

    Acoustic equipment, a thermal camera and pressure testing trace the leak to a spot on the floor plan. We mark it before any cutting.

  3. Choose a repair path

    Spot repair, reroute or repipe, covered below. We explain what each would mean for your floors and for your next leak.

  4. Open and repair

    Concrete is cut or the new line is run, depending on the choice. Pressure is tested before anything is closed up.

  5. Restore and dry out

    Cut sections are patched, and wet materials are dried or removed. Flooring work after concrete is usually separate.

Spot Repair, Reroute or Repipe: Honest Trade-Offs

OptionBest whenTrade-off
Spot repair through the slabOne leak, the rest of the line is sound, and the leak is reachable.Cutting concrete is dusty and noisy. Other weak spots in old copper can leak later.
Reroute through walls or atticThe line is old and the slab path is hard to reach.Walls or ceilings are opened and patched. New line is exposed in the cavity, not buried.
Repipe the affected loop or houseSeveral leaks, or old tubing across the whole home.Highest cost and most disruption now, with the least risk of repeat slab leaks.

A second leak within a short time on the same line is usually the sign to stop patching and read the repipe option seriously. See repiping for pipe materials and permits.

What to do before the leak locator arrives

A slab leak wastes water while you wait, so these steps limit damage and help the locator find the line faster.

  • ✓Shut off the house valve. ACWD says to turn the house valve clockwise. If water use at the meter stops, the leak is on your side.
  • ✓Keep water off the floor. Move rugs and boxes, and keep people off wet slab areas near outlets or appliances.
  • ✓Do not cut or drill the slab. The locator finds the line first, so the opening is small and in the right place.
  • ✓Note what you see and hear. Warm floor spots, the sound of running water and a spinning meter all help narrow the search.
