Before the pipe

Planning comes before pipe

A good job is decided before the first cut. We look at the existing drain size and slope, where the vent runs, how far the new fixture sits from the stack, and what is behind the wall. A bathroom added at the far end of the house can mean a longer drain run, which affects venting and slope.

Older homes bring their own questions. ACWD says homes built before 1960 are likely to have galvanized service lines, and a remodel is a good time to see whether the supply is still adequate before new fixtures are added to it.

We also talk through fixtures themselves. Faucets, showerheads and toilets carry flow limits under the California code, so the models you pick decide the water use, not us.

Permits, inspections and the Union City Building Division

The city says a plumbing permit is needed for most construction. Stopping a leak or clearing a stoppage does not need one, but replacing or rearranging pipes, valves or fixtures can, and replacing concealed pipe is treated as new work.

Ask the Building Division at 510.675.5313 about your project before you start. Counter hours are Monday through Thursday, 9 to 5, with limited Friday hours. For inspections, the line is 510.487.0711, and the city says to call early because slots are limited each day. Union City has adopted the 2025 California codes, effective January 1, 2026, so ask which edition applies when your plans are dated.

Source type: City of Union City Building Division and California Building Standards Commission

Sequence

How a rough-in and set-up unfolds

  1. Site walk and fixture list

    We confirm the fixtures, the rough-in dimensions and the location of existing lines before we price the work.

  2. Permit check

    We verify what the Building Division requires and schedule the work around inspections.

  3. Rough-in

    Drains, vents and supply lines go in, then get pressure tested before anyone closes a wall.

  4. Inspection

    The city inspects the open work where a permit is required. Walls stay open until it is signed off.

  5. Trim and test

    After tile and cabinets, we set the fixtures, connect traps and stops, and test every valve and drain.

Double vanity

Plumbing a double vanity: what to know

A double vanity is a common request, and the plumbing is not just two of everything.

  • ✓Each sink normally gets its own trap. Sharing one is limited by code and a common cause of gurgling.
  • ✓The drains combine and vent to code. Both sinks tie into one drain line with venting sized for the fixtures.
  • ✓Supply stops for both. That means four quarter-turn valves, each in reach.
  • ✓Faucet spacing matters. Choose the faucets and sinks before we rough in, so the centers line up with the drains.
  • ✓Plan for access. A false back or a removable panel makes future repairs easier.
