Act first

The toilet is overflowing right now

Do not flush again. Reach behind the bowl, turn the chrome stop valve clockwise until it seats, and take the lid off the tank. If the valve is stuck or the water keeps coming, shut the whole house off at the main valve (clockwise, per ACWD).

Overflow water from a toilet is contaminated, so keep children and pets out and use gloves for cleanup. A plunger with a flange (the soft rubber sleeve) is the right tool for a bowl, and a flat cup plunger is not.

If a second toilet, a tub or a floor drain also gurgles or backs up, the blockage is probably in the main line, not the bowl. That is a sewer job, and the plunger will not help.

Symptoms

What the toilet is telling you

Runs or hisses after flushing

Usually a worn flapper that no longer seals, a chain with too much slack, or a fill valve that will not shut off. Water is slipping into the bowl all day.

Refills by itself every so often

Called a phantom flush. The flapper leaks just enough to drop the tank level until the fill valve kicks in.

Weak or half flush

Check the tank water line against the mark on the overflow tube, then the chain length. If both are fine, mineral buildup in the rim jets or a partial clog is the likelier cause.

Water around the base

The wax ring or flange has failed, or the closet bolts have loosened. Water behind the toilet can also be a cracked supply line or a sweating tank, so find the source first.

Gurgles after you flush

A partial clog or a venting problem. If the bathroom sink or tub also burps when the toilet drains, the line past the toilet needs a look.

Toilet leaking through the floor?

Describe where the water shows up and we will help you decide whether to shut the stop valve or the whole house off.

The visit

How a toilet repair visit goes

  1. Find the water source

    We dry the base and tank, then watch where the first drop appears. Supply line, tank bolts, gasket and wax ring all look alike until the area is dry.

  2. Test the tank

    Dye in the tank, no flush, wait. ACWD suggests 20 minutes, and color in the bowl proves a silent leak at the flapper or flush valve seat.

  3. Replace the failing parts

    Fill valve, flapper, chain, supply line or tank gaskets. We prefer swapping the fill valve and flapper together, because both wear at the same pace.

  4. Reset the seal if needed

    To fix a leaking base we pull the toilet, scrape the old wax, check the flange and fit a new ring. A flange sitting below the finished floor gets a spacer or extender.

  5. Flush and test

    We run several flushes and watch the base for seepage before we call it done.

Decision guide

Repair the toilet or replace it

Parts are cheap compared with a fixture, but a bad toilet is a bad investment however many parts you add.

Union City water and the toilet in your house

The 2025 California codes adopted by Union City cap new residential toilets at 1.28 gallons per flush, so any replacement installed here will be a 1.28 gpf model. Choose by flush performance, since the lower volume gives a weak design less room to hide.

Your water is also moderately hard (ACWD reports 120 to 160 ppm for the blended supply, average 138, about 8 grains per gallon). Scale builds up in the fill valve seat and the rim jets, so a toilet that flushes weakly may need descaling before you condemn it.

Before you call

Quick checks that save a trip

  • ✓Drop food coloring in the tank. If it shows in the bowl after 20 minutes without flushing, the flapper or its seat is leaking.
  • ✓Watch the tank level. If water spills into the overflow tube, the float is set too high or the fill valve is failing.
  • ✓Note which fixtures react. A toilet that bubbles when the washer drains points to the main drain line, so sewer line cleaning is the right call.

Installation

Installing a new toilet, step by step

A swap starts with the rough-in, the distance from the wall to the drain center. Twelve inches is the common measurement, but older homes sometimes have 10 or 14, and the new bowl has to match. We also check the flange height, the subfloor condition and the angle stop valve before the old toilet comes out.

A new wax ring, fresh closet bolts and a braided supply line go in with the new bowl. The bowl is set straight, snugged down evenly (porcelain cracks when one side is overtightened) and caulked at the floor so that a future leak is visible rather than hidden.

Ask the city Building Division whether a fixture replacement in your home needs a permit. The city says repairing fixtures does not, but replacing or rearranging them can.
