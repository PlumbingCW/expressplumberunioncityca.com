When a drain backs up in the middle of service

Stop adding water. Close the kitchen, stop the dish machine and turn off the sinks that feed the affected drain. A floor drain that rises is usually a sign the building drain or a grease line downstream is blocked.

Move product, paper goods and cash off the floor, and mark the wet area. Take photos for your records and any insurance claim, and call the 24/7 line before the flood spreads to the dining room or sales floor.

Do not pour chemicals into a blocked commercial drain. They sit in the pipe and are hazardous for whoever opens it next.

Grease

Grease trap and interceptor service

Commercial kitchens send fat, oil and grease into the sewer unless a trap or interceptor catches it. Neglect is the most common cause of restaurant drain backups.

  1. Know which device you have

    A small trap sits under or beside the sink and holds a few gallons. A large interceptor is buried outdoors or in the floor and holds hundreds.

  2. Clean on a schedule

    The trap works only while it has room. When the grease layer fills, the unit lets fat pass into the lines. Many kitchens clean small units weekly or monthly and have the outdoor unit pumped by a hauler.

  3. Inspect the baffles and lids

    A cracked baffle or a missing gasket lets grease slip through and let odors out. We check the internals each time we open it.

  4. Jet the line after it

    Grease that has already coated the pipe wall needs hydro jetting to remove it, since cabling only punches a hole.

Ask USD and your city what grease rules apply to your kitchen, because pumping frequency is set by local rules and by how much you cook.

Inside the building

Floor drains, restrooms and flushometers

Floor drains that rise

A floor drain is the lowest point in the system, so it shows a main line blockage first. Strainers also catch mop debris, so we clean and trap-prime them.

Dry traps and odors

A floor drain trap evaporates when it sees no water. A sewer smell in a seldom-used room is often a dry trap, and it is fixed by adding water or a trap primer.

Flushometer toilets

Wall-mounted valve toilets that flush on a button need clean diaphragms and proper supply volume. Weak flushes or constant running usually point to a worn diaphragm or an adjustment.

Mop sinks and hose bibs

These are heavy-use fixtures that wear out. Replacing a seat or stem often costs less than a leak.

Backflow testing and the ACWD annual rule

ACWD runs a cross-connection control program for Union City, and the law requires that backflow prevention assemblies be tested by a certified tester at least once a year. Restaurants with carbonated drinks, irrigation, fire lines or boilers are common candidates.

ACWD keeps a list of approved backflow testers and a backflow office at 510-668-6584. A failed assembly has to be repaired or replaced and retested, so book the test early.

Commercial plumbing is built and inspected to the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and some systems add their own requirements, such as grease interceptors and backflow assemblies. Ask your landlord or the city which rules apply to your space before ordering any work.

Rain and runoff

Storm drains and parking-lot catch basins

Storm drains carry rainwater, and nothing else should enter them. A mop bucket dumped in a lot catch basin, or kitchen wash water hosed into a gutter, is a pollution problem that follows the business owner. Wash water goes to the sanitary drain, not the storm drain.

The Fremont station averages about 16 inches of rain a year (NOAA normal), and most of it falls in a short wet season. Leaves and debris clog catch basins just when they are needed, so clear them before the first big storm. Standing water that never drains near a catch basin can signal a failing underground line, which leak detection can trace.

Prevention

A short list for managers and owners

  • ✓Know your shutoffs. Label the main water valve, the fixture valves and the grease device lids, and keep staff trained.
  • ✓Set a grease schedule. Write down the last pumping date and the next one.
  • ✓Test backflow on time. Keep the annual report on site.
  • ✓Run the floor drains. Add water to little-used drains monthly.
  • ✓Watch for slow drains. They are the early warning, and an early cleaning beats a closed dining room.

For repeating blockages, a sewer camera inspection shows what is happening inside the line. We also take calls across Fremont and the Tri-City area.

What to do before we arrive

For an after-hours call, a few minutes of action protects the space and the people in it.

  • ✓Close the water valve. Shut the valve serving the affected area or the main, and tell staff where it is.
  • ✓Keep people clear. Block off wet floors and restrooms that are backing up, and do not run more water down the drain.
  • ✓Do not pour chemicals. Drain chemicals can splash and complicate the work, so leave the clog as it is.
  • ✓Contact the property manager. If you lease the space, the landlord may own the line, so tell them and photograph the damage.
