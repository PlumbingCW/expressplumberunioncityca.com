Where Alvarado fits in Union City

Union City was incorporated in 1959 from the older communities of Alvarado, Decoto and New Haven, according to BART. Alvarado has the longest paper trail of the three. A California Historical Landmark at 30977 Union City Blvd marks the site of Alameda County's first courthouse, where county government began in 1853 before the seat moved to San Leandro in 1856.

The city does not publish one boundary line that every resident agrees on, so we describe Alvarado by its anchor streets. Union City Blvd and Smith Street meet at the historic center, and Alvarado-Niles Road leads toward City Hall at 34009 Alvarado-Niles Road. The city also names an Old Alvarado Historic District, and its Saturday farmers' market is held in the Alvarado Historic District.

If you are unsure whether your street counts as Alvarado, that does not change the plumbing. Water comes from the Alameda County Water District and sewer service from Union Sanitary District across all of Union City.

Historic district rules that can touch a plumbing job

Union City's code sets up a landmark and historic preservation zone. Inside it, a second-story addition to a historic residence or a new accessory structure over 120 square feet in a historic district needs administrative site development review first. The Historic Building Code also applies unless a structure is noncontributing.

Most plumbing runs inside walls and does not change how a house looks. A new detached laundry room, a bathroom in a backyard structure or an exterior vent is different, so ask city planning before you start. We could not find a public map of the exact district boundary, so the city is the place to confirm whether your address is covered.

The city also says stopping leaks, clearing stoppages and repairing fixtures do not need a permit, but replacing concealed defective pipe counts as new work and does. The Building Division is at 510.675.5313 if you are unsure which side of that line your job falls on.

Union Sanitary District

Sewer laterals: the pipe many owners do not know they own

Union Sanitary District serves sewer for Union City, and its rule is plain. The property owner is responsible for the entire lateral from the house to the district main, including the stretch under the sidewalk and street. If sewage backs up, USD asks you to call its 24 hour line at (510) 477-7500 so it can check its own main.

If the main is clear, the blockage is probably in your lateral. A cleanout just outside the building or behind the sidewalk is where a plumber gets in, and older homes may not have one. A camera inspection shows the pipe before you choose between cleaning the line and repairing it.

Any lateral repair needs a USD repair permit, even on private property. If the work reaches the street, the city adds an encroachment permit. Call 811 at least 48 hours before anyone digs.

Before you call: four things worth doing

1

Find the house valve

ACWD says to turn the house valve clockwise to shut off water to the property.

2

Watch the meter

Shut the valve and see whether the meter still moves. If it does, ACWD says the leak is likely outside the house.

3

Note what is slow

One slow fixture usually means a local clog. Several at once point toward the lateral.

4

Take photos

Stains, standing water and the cleanout cap help a plumber picture the job before arriving.
