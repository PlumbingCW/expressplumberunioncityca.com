The right first step depends on what you are looking at. Cold water from the hot tap is annoying but rarely dangerous. Water spreading on the floor is not, so close the cold supply to the heater and cut power or fuel before anything else.

Symptoms

What your heater is telling you

Lukewarm or runs out fast

On electric units, a dead lower element or thermostat. On gas, a weak burner or heavy sediment layer at the bottom of the tank.

Rumbling or popping

Scale on the bottom of the tank traps steam bubbles under it. This is common with hard water and responds to a flush if caught in time.

Rust-colored hot water

Usually a spent anode rod or a corroding tank. If only the hot side is discolored, the heater is the likely source.

Pilot will not stay lit

A worn thermocouple, a dirty pilot orifice, or a draft problem at the flue. Each has a different cure.

Dripping at the side or top

A loose fitting, a failing T&P relief valve, or the expansion of water in a closed system. Find the source before assuming the tank.

Water around the base

A leaking drain valve is a cheap fix. Water from the tank body is not repairable, see replacement.

Local water

Hard water in Union City and what it does to a tank

The Alameda County Water District reports that Union City receives a blended supply with hardness between 120 and 160 ppm, averaging 138 ppm, or about 8 grains per gallon. That is hard water by common scales. Minerals settle at the bottom of a tank, coat electric elements and insulate gas burners from the water.

The practical payoff is a simple routine. Flushing a tank once a year and checking the anode rod can delay the kind of failure that ends a tank early. Check your ACWD water quality report to see your own neighborhood's number.

Repairs such as relief valve and thermostat work follow the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) and the manufacturer's instructions. If a unit needs repeated repairs, ask whether the installation itself meets that standard before you pay for another part.

Anode rod

The part that decides how long a tank lasts

The anode rod sacrifices itself so the tank liner does not. When the rod is consumed, corrosion moves to the steel. Checking it every few years with a socket and a breaker bar is cheap, and replacing it can add years to a tank. We say honestly when it is too late.
