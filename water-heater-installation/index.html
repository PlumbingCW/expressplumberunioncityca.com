How installation differs from replacement and repair

Replacement swaps a failed tank in the same spot, using the same connections. Repair fixes a part on a working or partly working unit. Installation starts from a blank slate. A new location, a change in fuel, or a new building means someone must plan the venting, the supply lines, the drainage and the code items before the first fitting is made.

That planning is where the money and the headaches live. A unit moved from a garage to a hall closet needs different venting. A switch from gas to a heat pump needs a circuit and a condensate drain. Plan these before you buy the unit, not after it arrives.

Site requirements

What the location has to provide

Location and clearances

Gas units need combustion air and clearances from combustibles. Heat pump units need open air volume or ducting. Garage installs have extra rules for ignition source height.

Venting

Atmospheric gas units use a draft hood and a flue that rises and terminates correctly. Sealed-combustion and condensing models vent through plastic pipe to the side or roof.

Gas line

The line must be sized for the total gas load, with a shutoff, a sediment trap and a flexible connector. A longer run or an added appliance can call for a larger pipe.

Electric service

Electric and heat pump units need a dedicated circuit sized to the nameplate. An older panel may limit the choice.

Permits and codes

Union City's code year and the Building Division

Union City adopted the 2025 California codes, including the Plumbing and Mechanical codes, effective January 1, 2026. The City states that a building, plumbing, mechanical and electrical permit is needed for most types of construction. Planned installs, especially new locations or fuel changes, are the sort of work that tends to be inspected.

The practical payoff is a short phone call. Before you commit to a plan, call the Building Division at 510.675.5313 and ask how your project is handled. Inspection requests go through 510.487.0711, and the line has limited slots each day, so calling early helps.

Water heater installation follows the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), including the pressure relief valve, discharge piping and seismic strapping required by California law. Ask what will be included, because each item is a code requirement, not an upsell.

Sequence

From plan to a hot tap

  1. Site visit or detailed call

    We confirm location, clearances, existing venting, gas and electric capacity.

  2. Scope and code review

    You get the list of code items and the permit approach before work starts.

  3. Rough-in

    Supply, gas, vent and electrical are run or modified.

  4. Set and strap

    The unit is set in the pan, strapped and connected.

  5. Test and inspection

    We test for leaks, flame or power, relief discharge and expansion, then support the inspection.
