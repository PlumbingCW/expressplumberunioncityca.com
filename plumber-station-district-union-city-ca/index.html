Plumber in the Station District, Union City, CA

The Station District is the planning area around the Union City BART station. It mixes existing buildings with new development and land still to be developed, so it is not a traditional single-family neighborhood.

This page is written for building managers, owners and tenants, with the documented facts about the district and plumbing guidance for multi-unit and commercial buildings.

The area

What the Station District is

Union City's planning documents describe the Station District as an area of about 471 acres anchored on the Union City Intermodal Station, which serves BART, UC Transit, AC Transit and the Dumbarton Express. The station is at 10 Union Square. The plan sets out five subareas: the Core, Station East, the Marketplace, the Gateway and the Civic Center.

The Station District Specific Plan replaced the Decoto Industrial Park Study Area plan from 1994. At full buildout in 2040, the plan anticipates about 5,650 housing units, of which 3,930 would be new. Those are planning figures and not what stands today, so many parcels in the district are still being developed.

Tenant or building owner: who handles what

General guidance, since leases and associations differ. Your lease or association rules decide in the end.

If you rent a unit

  • Report a leak or backup to your manager at once, in writing if you can.
  • Know where your unit's shutoff or the building's master valve is.
  • Do not try to open shared drain lines or valves you do not control.
  • Ask who authorizes outside plumbers before you call one.

If you own or manage the building

  • Keep a map of shutoffs, risers and cleanouts where staff can find it.
  • Test shutoff valves on a schedule so they turn when needed.
  • Know which fixtures drain to a shared line and which to a private one.
  • Keep records of backflow tests and water heater work.

For a leak or stoppage that reaches several units, a leak detection visit or a drain cleaning run on the shared line is the first step.

What the city's environmental review says about the ground

The environmental review places the planning area west of the Hayward Fault, with the closest trace about 680 feet northeast. It describes moderate to very high liquefaction susceptibility, and says expansive soils underlie most of the Station East, Core and Civic Center subareas.

That review is about construction planning and says nothing about any one building's plumbing. The practical takeaway is simple. Know where every shutoff is, check that water heaters are strapped as California law requires, and ask a plumber about flexible connections where pipes meet the building.

Source type: Union City Station District environmental impact report, geology chapter

ACWD

Backflow devices and cross-connections

ACWD requires backflow assemblies to be tested at least once a year by a certified tester, and it may ask a customer to install a device at the meter after a survey. The district keeps a list of approved testers who are local plumbing contractors. Its cross-connection control ordinance was adopted in 2025.

Restaurants, irrigation systems, fire lines and buildings with a boiler or backup water system are common places for these devices. Call ACWD's backflow office at 510-668-6584 if you are unsure whether yours is due. Our commercial plumbing page lists what we handle for businesses.

Permits

Permits and who pulls them

The city says a plumbing permit is needed for most types of construction and lists exceptions for repairs such as stopping leaks. It has adopted the 2025 California Plumbing Code. The Building Division is at 510.675.5313, and permits for a commercial or multi-unit job are worth confirming before work starts.

California requires a contractor's license for work valued at one thousand dollars or more in labor and materials. Ask whoever you hire who is pulling the permit and who schedules the inspection.
