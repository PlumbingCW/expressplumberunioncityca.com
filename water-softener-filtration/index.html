Water Softener and Filtration in Union City, CA

Union City water is hard, and many homes see scale on fixtures and inside heaters. A softener treats hardness. A filter treats other things. We explain which is which, install both, and tell you what neither can do.

Your water

What the Alameda County Water District reports

The Alameda County Water District serves Union City with a blended supply. Hardness runs from 120 to 160 ppm as calcium carbonate, with an average of 138 ppm. ACWD says to divide by 17.1 to get grains per gallon, so the average is about 8 grains per gallon. The district also reports that the disinfectant is chloramines.

The practical payoff is that your decision starts with the number, not a sales pitch. Hardness explains scale. Chloramines explain why a plain filter may not change the taste. ACWD has a hardness map, so check your neighborhood before you buy equipment.

Softener and filter installs follow the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), including backflow protection and a proper drain connection. Ask the installer how the unit is protected against backflow and where the discharge goes.

Source type: Alameda County Water District 2025 water quality report

Softeners

What a softener actually does

A salt-regenerated softener swaps calcium and magnesium for sodium or potassium on a resin bed. The resin gets recharged with brine, and the brine flushes to a drain. The result is soft water that protects water heaters, fixtures and glass from scale.

A softener does not make water safer to drink, and it does not remove chloramines. It needs a drain, power, salt refills and a plan for the brine discharge. Ask USD, the sewer agency, whether any local limits apply to softener discharge.

Honest comparison

Salt softener versus salt-free conditioner

Salt-based softener

  • Removes hardness minerals from the water
  • Reduces scale on heaters, fixtures and glass
  • Needs salt, power and a drain
  • Makes a brine discharge to the sewer

Salt-free conditioner

  • Changes how minerals form scale, but does not remove them
  • No salt, no brine discharge
  • Results vary, especially on the hardest water
  • Does not give the slick feel of softened water

Where hardness is about 8 grains per gallon, a true softener is the option that actually lowers hardness. Salt-free units reduce scale in some conditions, but they do not soften.

Limits

What filtration cannot do

No filter or softener replaces the testing and treatment done by the water district. We make no health claims beyond what the ACWD report says. If you have a specific concern, such as a medical need or an unusual taste, get your tap water tested by a state-certified lab.

A softener will not fix low pressure, a rusty pipe or a leaking fixture. For those, see leak detection or repiping.

Before you buy

How to test your tap first

  • ✓Read the ACWD report. Find your area's hardness on the district's map or report.
  • ✓Use a test strip for a quick check. Hardness strips cost little and show whether scale matches the number.
  • ✓Look at the symptoms. White crust on fixtures points to hardness. A chlorine smell points to disinfectant. Cloudiness points to air or particles.
  • ✓Decide what you want. Scale protection, better drinking taste, or both.
  • ✓Ask about maintenance. Salt refills, filter changes and RO membranes all have schedules.
Install

How a softener is plumbed in

  1. Pick the location

    Near the main shutoff with a drain and power nearby. A bypass valve is part of the plan.

  2. Plumb the loop

    Hot and cold supply lines are arranged so outdoor hose bibs and the kitchen cold tap can be left unsoftened if you choose.

  3. Run the drain

    The brine line needs an air gap and a proper discharge point.

  4. Program and test

    Hardness, capacity and regeneration schedule are set, and the system is tested for leaks.

A softener extends the life of a tankless unit and tank heaters, though it does not replace flushing. Our crew also serves Fremont, see plumbing in Fremont.
